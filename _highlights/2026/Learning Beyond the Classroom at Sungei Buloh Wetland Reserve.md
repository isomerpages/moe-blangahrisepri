<p>Our Primary 6 students recently embarked on an enriching Science Learning
Journey to Sungei Buloh Wetland Reserve, where they experienced Singapore's
unique mangrove ecosystem up close. Guided by the knowledgeable facilitators
from iNature, students journeyed through the reserve, carefully observing
the diverse plants and wildlife in their natural habitat.</p>
<p>As they encountered mangrove trees, mudskippers, fiddler crabs, birds
and other fascinating organisms, students witnessed firsthand the adaptations
they had previously learnt about in the classroom. One of the highlights
of the learning journey was encountering a magnificent Golden Orb Weaver
spinning its intricate web among the trees. The experience deepened their
understanding of biodiversity and conservation while demonstrating how
Science can be observed in the real world.</p>
<p></p>

<p>Reflecting on her learning journey, Minh Anh from 6 Opal shared, <em>"It was amazing to actually hear the cicadas suddenly fall silent when rain was approaching. I had learnt that animals can sense changes in the environment, but experiencing it for myself made it so memorable."</em> Her
reflection captures how being immersed in nature allowed students to make
meaningful connections between classroom learning and authentic observations.</p>
<p>Similarly, Ghim Chiu from 6 Topaz reflected, <em>"I was surprised by how much life existed in the mangroves. Looking closely at the plants and animals in their natural habitat helped me understand how each living thing is specially adapted to survive. It made me appreciate nature in a way I never had before."</em>
</p>

<p>Through this experiential learning opportunity, our students not only
strengthened their understanding of adaptation and ecosystems but also
developed a deeper appreciation for Singapore's rich biodiversity. The
learning journey inspired curiosity, keen observation and a stronger sense
of environmental stewardship, reinforcing that some of the most meaningful
lessons take place beyond the classroom.</p>
