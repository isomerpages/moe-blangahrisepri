<h4><strong>Teaching and Learning</strong></h4>
<p>In our Malay Language classes, a variety of strategies are adopted to
enhance students’ learning and to provide them with opportunities to demonstrate
their thinking and communication skills. Students not only learn through
textbooks, but also through experiential learning, which helps to make
learning meaningful and engaging. For instance, the infusion of Malay literature
elements such as <em>pantun</em>, <em>puisi</em> and <em>cerpen</em> enables
students to develop a deeper understanding of the language while learning
more about Malay culture. Through these experiences, students develop a
greater appreciation of both the language and the culture, and are encouraged
to share their joy of learning the Malay Language with others.</p>
<p>In addition, ICT-based lessons are thoughtfully designed to support and
enrich the learning process. Digital tools and applications are used purposefully
to complement classroom instruction, provide timely feedback and offer
students opportunities to learn more independently, thereby value-adding
to their overall learning experience.</p>

<p><strong>Malay Language Fortnight and Camps</strong>
</p>
<p>Our annual Malay Language Fortnight and Malay Language Camps provide meaningful
and authentic platforms for students to learn and use the Malay Language
while deepening their understanding and appreciation of Malay culture and
traditions. Through these programmes, students are given frequent opportunities
to use the language in real-life contexts and to engage in experiential
learning beyond the classroom.</p>
<p>The Malay Language Camps, conducted for Primary 4 students, immerse learners
in authentic settings where they participate in a range of learning experiences
centred on the Malay Language and culture. Similarly, during the Malay
Language Fortnight, students across all levels take part in fun-filled
cultural activities and Malay Language reading sessions that encourage
active use of the language.</p>

<p>Students are also immersed in rich cultural experiences such as Inang
dance, kompang and gamelan performances, which further enhance their appreciation
of the language, culture and traditions, and strengthen their confidence
in using the Malay Language meaningfully.</p>
<p><strong>Geng Sahibba<em><br></em></strong>Geng Sahibba (Malay Scrabble)
provides students with a platform to apply their language knowledge and
widen their vocabulary. Primary 3 to Primary 6 students who have a flair
for the Malay language are selected to join the Geng Sahibba sessions,
where they learn game strategies and thinking skills. Students will be
selected to represent the school in zonal and national competitions. Students
learn to be resilient, proactive and good team players.</p>

<p><strong>Rakan Bahasa Programme</strong>
</p>
<p>The Rakan Bahasa programme by Majlis Bahasa Melayu, Singapura (MBMS) encourages
students who are passionate about the Malay Language to serve as role models
in promoting the use of the language among their family and friends. Through
the programme, newly appointed Rakan Bahasa representatives are provided
with opportunities to participate in networking and training sessions,
where they learn from peers from other schools and develop greater confidence
in carrying out their roles as language ambassadors.</p>

<p><strong>MTL Challenge (Malay Language)<br></strong>Learning takes place
when students are able to apply what they have learnt in the classroom.
As such, the MTL Challenge (Malay Language) is organised for our students
every semester. These internal competitions range from speaking to writing,
providing students with opportunities to showcase their creativity and
apply their language knowledge in meaningful ways.</p>
